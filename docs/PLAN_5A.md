# Phase 5a: the story revision (plan, proposed 2026-10-09)

Design only (decision D10): no code, no scenes, no dialogue database edits. 5a revises Acts II–IV for the inn-and-village direction, using the material GDD §2.4 and §2.9 set aside for revision, and settles enough of the story that every later Phase 5 milestone has a reason in it. Nothing here is built; the Phase 5 sequence (GDD §11.1) stays a planning baseline.

## 1. The experience the story should create

**Experience target:** *The keeper comes to Kariaston as a stranger running a dead woman's tavern, and becomes the person the village trusts to go below; every act, the Hollows ask more of them and give more back, and the village grows because of what the keeper brings up, while the truth of the seal, Karias and Phi comes out through the people who carry it.*

Two halves, kept in tension (pillar 4, *cozy on the surface, dread below*): the story's danger lives below and in the people it has marked, and it reaches the village as consequence and mood, not as sieges. Lenses: **Character web** (each beat moves more than one person), **Interest curve** (an act has a rise and a peak, the peaks are bosses and revelations), **Story machine** (the systems, the Inn, the plots, the calendar, the biomes, generate their own small stories inside the authored spine).

## 3. Deliverables

All in `docs/` (no `Assets/` changes):

- **A mapping from story to Phase 5:** for 5b–5i, the story reason each milestone carries (for example, which act's arrivals the Inn and the plots serve, what a first festival remembers, which biome holds which revelation), so system milestones and story stay one plan. ## 4. How it runs: two checkpoints

**Checkpoint A, the spine (a short document, then the owner's choices).** Two or three distinct directions for the story's foundation, each a page: what the source beyond the seal is and what the Warden Below is; what happened to Phi and why she went down; the Hollows' reach; how the story ends (what the keeper does at the Heart, and what the village is afterwards); how much menace reaches the surface. Each direction says what it does to every act, every member of the cast and the Phase 5 milestones, so the choice is between whole stories, not loose answers. The directions are built from the locked canon and the hooks already in the game (Phi's two trips after the Larder Troll and "whatever it was sitting on", Kaloren's phylactery, Ogrin found beside two dead adults, Glimmer the failed guardian, Maximo who cannot go back, Toshi below, Musashi's curse, Gimp's arrangement with Phi and his hatred of Maximo). **Stop for the owner's pick** (or a mix).

**Checkpoint B, the acts (the story bible).** On the chosen spine: Acts II–IV in full beat outlines, the cast's arcs, every §2.9 conflict resolved, the open questions down to the ones the owner still wants open, the Phase 5 mapping, and the proposed GDD changes as a diff. **Stop for the owner's review**; the GDD changes go in only after sign-off.

The owner's sign-off on Checkpoint B closes 5a (no build, no playtest; the "playtest" of a story revision is the owner reading it). Tag: none (design-only milestones aren't tagged; the next tag is the next build milestone's).

## 5. Principles the revision follows

- **The village grows naturally** (D10): more people, more rooms, a bigger property, village improvements, new arrivals, consequences of delving; never a fortress or a war camp. Newcomers arrive as Visitors, guests and residents (the systems 5d and 5f build), not as refugees on a wall, unless a specific beat earns it.
- **The menace stays mostly below.** The surface feels the Hollows through people (the marked, the missing, the returned), things brought up, and the occasional consequence that is personal rather than military. Escalation means the Hollows asking more of the keeper and the village caring more about what happens down there.
- **The cast carries the story.** Revelations come through people, objects and places, in their own voices (CLAUDE.md: never a cosmology dump; Phi's history comes out gradually). Every act gives the learning pillars work: conversations that branch, people who remember, odd and funny moments, things for the property, dishes worth cooking for someone.
- **Gating by depth and by people.** Acts advance by depth in the Hollows and by village and inn milestones (people settled, rooms opened, relationships, quests), never by a calendar date, and nothing story-critical is missable.
- **Systems serve the story, not the reverse.** No new mechanic is invented in 5a. Where a beat seems to want one (a festival, a ritual, a vote), it is noted for the milestone that would build it, with the smallest version that would serve.
- **The scale fits a life sim.** A region and a village, the people in it, and what lies under them; the world beyond Aldmere's borders stays rumour unless the owner wants more.

## 6. What the owner does

- Read Checkpoint A and choose a direction (or a mix), and answer whichever of the open questions you want settled now.
- Read Checkpoint B and sign off, change or reject parts of it.
- Point me at anything from your own notes or campaigns you want the story to draw on (as with the Fortunate Five and Musashi: what serves the characters, never the campaign wholesale). The campaign notes aren't on this machine.

## 7. Risks

- **Closing doors too early.** A spine fixes the ending; Phase 5 will take a long time. Each direction says which choices it locks and which it leaves open, and anything the owner isn't ready to lock stays open.
- **Scope creep from story.** A good act can imply systems (new areas, a festival per act, a new kind of NPC). The mapping proposes; each milestone's plan decides.
- **Contradicting what's built.** Act I, Boog's Bomb, Gimp's night, Orik's lines and the 4h cast are canon in the game; the revision builds on them and lists any line it would want changed as an author's edit for later, never silently.

## 8. Gate

Two stops: after Checkpoint A (the owner chooses the spine) and after Checkpoint B (the owner signs off the story bible and the GDD changes). The playtest's feedback (`docs/PLAYTEST_4I.md`) doesn't bind 5a, but anything a tester says about the story, the cast or the tone is read in at Checkpoint B.
