# 5a Checkpoint A: the story's spine (for the owner's choice, 2026-10-09)

Design only (`docs/PLAN_5A.md`). Three whole directions for what lies beneath Kariaston, what happened to Phi, who the Warden Below is and how the story ends; then each open difference and each idea of mine as a choice with its cost and a recommendation. **Nothing here is canon until the owner chooses.** Nothing about Gimp and Maximo goes into dialogue until then.

The owner's two campaigns are the source (kept outside the repo): the earlier one gives the founding generation (Maximo, Karias, Gimp, with Boog as their friend), the later one the Fortunate Five (Phi, Grim, Musashi, Bart and their friends). What comes across is adapted to Aldmere and the Hollows; other settings' names stay out, and only the owner's own songs are used. Where a campaign person or thing has no game name yet it's written as a placeholder in brackets ([the betrayer], [Phi's spear]); the owner may reuse their own names for them.

**What every direction keeps:** the founding as locked (a source of evil opened beneath the region, a great war, Karias died sealing it with Maximo among the sealers, the imperfect seal's seepage is the Hollows, Maximo founded Kariaston as a watch); D10 (no fortress, defense or war camp); Act I as built; the cast's locked details; menace that lives mostly below; acts gated by depth and by people, never by date.

## 1. The three directions

### A. The Old Party (both campaigns joined)

**Experience:** *the keeper slowly realises the odd old people of Kariaston are the heroes of a war nobody sings about properly any more, and that their story isn't finished: the keeper is the one who gets to finish it, with the village instead of an army.*

- **The founding is the old party's last battle.** Centuries ago a catastrophe tore the world open beneath this region: a wound with a black, rooted heart that poured darkness up into the land and started the war. A party of odd companions fought their way to it: **Maximo** (a tanner's son who took up adventuring late, a romantic who believed in heroes), **Karias** (his apprentice, the bright young wizard who darkened as the war went on), **Gimp** (a ranger, and the best shot they had), **Boog** (a goblin with a pistol and too much powder, everyone's friend) and others the story can name later. At the heart, Karias sealed the wound and was lost. The seal held, imperfectly; the Hollows grew from what still seeps through.
- **The Fortunate Five are the next generation.** Long after, when Kariaston was a village and Tally Ho! a wreck over the hatch, five delvers (Phi, Grim, Musashi and two more; choice 2 below) went down into what the sealing left behind, and came back with more stories than they told. Phi rebuilt Tally Ho! with Orik, left, came home decades later, and went down nine days before the keeper arrived.
- **Why Phi went down:** her own past came looking for her. [The betrayer], a drow from her homeland, the reason she was exiled (she tried to stop his rituals; everyone with her died; he fled and she was blamed), came to Kariaston because the wound's power is under it. Phi recognised his work below (the Larder Troll was guarding something he'd left), went after him twice, and the second time didn't come back. She has a son at home she has never been able to return to.
- **The Warden Below:** two readings to choose between (A1 or A2):
  - **A1, the wound's voice.** What lies beyond the seal has a will; "the Warden" is what the delvers call the thing that speaks from it. [The betrayer] thinks he's using it; it's using him.
  - **A2, Karias.** Karias didn't simply die: to make the seal hold he put himself into it, and what is left of him has kept it for centuries, alone, slowly going wrong. The Warden Below is what Karias became: a keeper who no longer remembers why. A fragment of the old Karias, the one who held his staff "like a wish come true", still reaches out to the keeper through the deep. (This draws on the later campaign's unfinished vault, whose builder went mad keeping it and sealed his helpers in with him.) **Glimmer** was one of the fey spirits Karias called into the seal; she slipped out, damaged and forgetful, and the nearest living thing was an infant beside two dead delvers in the Hollows: Ogrin. She has been half-living in him since; she is why he grew ten years in two.
- **The ending:** at the Heart, [the betrayer] tries to take the wound's power and is taken by it instead. The keeper renews the seal, but not the old way: no one has to be lost in it. The binding passes from the old party to the living village (Maximo's creed below), which is what Morale and Cheer have meant all along. With A2, Karias is finally let go, and Maximo goes below once, to say goodbye.
- **The surface:** moderate. The seal's weakening shows up as personal, strange events (a night of restless spirits in Tally Ho!, the herbs failing, Ogrin's bad days), never sieges.
- **Acts:** **II** (Biomes 2–3): the old party comes into focus (Boog's and Gimp's past, Maximo's songs that aren't quite true), a drow is seen below, Phi's trail; Glimmer first speaks through Ogrin. **III** (Biomes 4–5): Phi found alive and freed, [Phi's spear] broken; the truth about the sealing (and, in A2, about Karias); Gimp and Maximo's reckoning. **IV** (Biome 6, 7 and the Heart): the village becomes the anchor; [the betrayer] at the Heart; the seal renewed; the old party let go.
- **Phase 5 hooks:** 5b's first festival can be a remembrance day for Karias (Maximo's speech, Gimp's absence); 5d's Inn brings [the betrayer] as a Visitor before anyone knows him, and later Phi's son; 5f's settlers include people the keeper brings up from below.
- **Locks:** the founding's details, Phi's past, the antagonist. **Leaves open:** the old party's other members, the betrayer's end, Toshi, Kaloren's phylactery.

### B. The Unfinished Prison (closest to the later campaign)

**Experience:** *the deeper the keeper goes, the more the Hollows stop looking like caves and start looking like something built: a prison that was never finished, and the keeper is the one who learns what it was built to hold.*

- **What lies below:** long before Kariaston, an ancient warden began a prison beneath the region for things too dangerous to destroy (call them banes: creatures and objects held outside time). Fey spirits wove life into its wards. The builder went mad before the last locks were set and sealed everything in, the fey with it. The great war was banes breaking out through the unfinished side; the old party sealed that breach (the founding as locked; Karias died doing it). The Hollows are the prison's outer works grown wild with seepage; each biome is a ward ring, each boss a jailer, an escapee or a guardian; the Heart is the innermost cell.
- **The Warden Below:** the builder, become something worse, with one remorseful fragment that warns the keeper about himself.
- **Why Phi went down:** the wards are failing (the Larder Troll was sitting on a ward stone), and [the betrayer] is below, opening cells to reach one bane he was promised power by. Phi went after him and is caught in a cell outside time: alive, unchanged, unaware nine days (or more) have passed.
- **Glimmer** is one of the sealed-in fey, the only one who got out; Ogrin's link is as in A.
- **The ending:** [the betrayer] takes the bane and is taken by it; the keeper, with Glimmer (through [Phi's spear], which she makes her new anchor so Ogrin can be free of her), completes the last ward. A choice at the end: finish the prison, keep it as it is, or break it for good, each with its own epilogue.
- **The surface:** low to moderate; escaped banes are rumours and one or two story nights.
- **Acts:** **II**: the Hollows reveal they were built (Kaloren knows too much about how; the Five's maps); a jailer boss. **III**: Phi found in a cell; the betrayer's goal; the fragment of the builder. **IV**: the bane, the last ward, the choice.
- **Locks:** a built underworld (every biome after the Cellars gets a ward's architecture and a reason); a final choice with endings. **Leaves open:** what the banes are one by one, Toshi, the old party's details.
- **Cost:** the most new lore to reveal, and the most at risk of a cosmology lecture; it pulls the Hollows' feel toward a dungeon with a plan.

### C. The Watch (closest to the locked founding; the campaigns supply only the people)

**Experience:** *the Hollows are a wound that never quite healed, and the story is about the people it has hurt: the keeper goes down night after night to bring back what the Hollows took, one person at a time.*

- **What lies below:** the founding exactly as locked and nothing more: a wound with no face. No mastermind, no prison. The seal frays with time, and with every delve (the cost of what the keeper does).
- **The Warden Below:** retired as a person: "the Warden" becomes what Kariaston calls the deepest dark, a name, never a character. (Glimmer's question 4 goes away.)
- **Why Phi went down:** a promise. The Five swore to find Toshi, Musashi's brother, who went deeper than any of them; Phi went back for him when she heard something below that sounded like him. She's found in Act III alive but emptied: the Hollows take memory (Kaloren's and Glimmer's through-line), and she doesn't know Tally Ho!, Orik or her own portrait. Act IV gives it back.
- **The campaigns' people, without their plots:** Maximo, Karias, Gimp and Boog were companions in the war; the Five delved after; their histories are anecdotes, regrets and quests, not a conspiracy.
- **The ending:** the seal is renewed by the village together (each act's people helping), and Maximo goes down one last time. Quiet, warm, smaller.
- **The surface:** low; the story is personal.
- **Acts:** **II**: Toshi's trail; Musashi's taste; the first recovered person. **III**: Phi found, without her memory. **IV**: the seal renewed; what everyone got back, and what they didn't.
- **Locks:** the least. **Leaves open:** nearly everything about the source.
- **Cost:** the weakest antagonist and climax (no one to face at the Heart unless the wound itself becomes a boss); the least use of the campaigns.

### Recommendation

**A, with A2.** It uses both campaigns as the owner asked, keeps the locked founding word for word, and answers the most open questions with the fewest new rules: why Maximo can't go back, Glimmer and the Warden, Ogrin's ageing, Gimp and Maximo, Boog and Gimp's bond, Phi's disappearance. It's the best fit for the learning priorities: a whole cast with shared history to write, reactivity between old friends, and a final act whose "army" is the village the player built (pillar 3, Character web and Story machine lenses). B is the strongest structure for biomes and bosses but the heaviest lore; C is the safest but the thinnest. A's risk is that A2's twist (Karias in the seal) is big; A1 keeps A without it.
